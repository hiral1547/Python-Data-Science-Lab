# Measures of Central Tendency, Dispersion, and Location

This Python program analyzes a dataset using basic statistical measures.

## 1. Central Tendency

It tells us the **center or typical value** of the data.

- **Mean** – Average of all values.
- **Median** – Middle value when data is sorted.
- **Mode** – Most frequently occurring value.

**Real-world use:** Finding average salary, average marks, or most commonly purchased product.

## 2. Dispersion

It tells us **how spread out the data is**.

- **Range** – Difference between maximum and minimum.
- **Variance** – Measures how far values are from the mean.
- **Standard Deviation** – Shows how much values typically vary from the mean.

**Real-world use:** Measuring consistency of exam scores, product quality, sales, or employee performance.

## 3. Measures of Location

They tell us **where a value lies within the dataset**.

- **Q1** – 25% of data is below this value.
- **Q2** – 50% of data is below this value (Median).
- **Q3** – 75% of data is below this value.
- **IQR** – Difference between Q3 and Q1.

**Real-world use:** Comparing exam rankings, salaries, house prices, or customer spending.

## In Simple Words

- **Central Tendency:** What is the typical value?
- **Dispersion:** How spread out is the data?
- **Location:** Where does a value stand compared to the rest?

These concepts are commonly used in **data analysis, business, statistics, finance, and machine learning**.
